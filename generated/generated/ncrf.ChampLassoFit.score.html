ncrf.ChampLassoFit.score¶

ChampLassoFit.score(forward, data)¶

ChampLasso’s likelihood objective on whitened data, and its residual term.

Both scores are averaged over the segments of data, and lower is better. For a segment with whitened sensor data \(y\) and model prediction \(\hat y\), let \(r = y - \hat y\) be the whitened residual and \(\Sigma_b\) the segment’s sigma_b:

weighted_l2_error

The squared residual weighted by the inverse of the estimated data covariance, \(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_b^{-1} r r^\top)\). Compared with the model metric l2_error, \(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(r r^\top)\), the residual is additionally scaled down in sensor directions that the model attributes to source activity.

cross_fit

The weighted residual plus the log-determinant term, \(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_b^{-1} r r^\top) + \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\Sigma_b\). This is the Gaussian type-II likelihood cost function that Champagne minimizes [Das et al., 2020], without the \(\ell_1\) penalty on the TRF, which makes it comparable across values of mu. It is the criterion by which ChampLasso selects mu in cross-validation.

On the training data (NCRFFit.scores) both reflect how well the final TRF and covariance estimates account for the data they were fitted to. On held-out data (CVResult.scores) the residual comes from the held-out window while \(\Sigma_b\) was estimated on the training folds, so the scores measure how well the fitted covariance model generalizes.
